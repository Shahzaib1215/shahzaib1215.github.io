5 Best Free Calendar Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Every Android phone already has a calendar — so why install another one? Because the built-in app is not always the best fit: some calendars handle shared family schedules badly, some have no proper task view, and some bury your events under a cluttered design. We tested the most popular free calendar apps on a mid-range Android phone for one question: which one keeps your day organised without a subscription?

What we tested

  • Everyday planning — adding events fast, recurring events, reminders that actually fire
  • Views & widgets — month, week, agenda views and a home-screen widget you can read at a glance
  • Sharing — family or team calendars, shared events, and calendar sync across devices
  • Free-tier honesty — what is genuinely free, and what quietly needs a paid account

1. Google Calendar — best for most people

Google Calendar is the safe default, and for good reason: it syncs everywhere, integrates with Gmail (flight and booking confirmations can appear automatically), and its widgets and agenda view are clean. Tasks are built in, reminders work reliably, and it is completely free for personal use. If you already use a Google account, start here — most third-party calendars are just a different skin over the same Google Calendar data.

2. Microsoft Outlook — best calendar + email in one app

If your work or study runs on Outlook email, its calendar is the natural companion: email and calendar in one app, a focused inbox, and shared calendars that work well with colleagues on Microsoft 365. The calendar itself is fully usable free with a Microsoft account; some advanced business features need a paid plan, but personal scheduling does not.

3. Samsung Calendar — best if you own a Samsung phone

Samsung Calendar is pre-installed on Galaxy phones and is genuinely good: clean month and week views, stickers and custom colours for event types, and solid sync with Google and Microsoft accounts. If you already have it, check it before installing anything else — it covers most needs with no ads and no account beyond the ones you already use.

4. TimeTree — best shared family calendar

TimeTree is built around sharing: one calendar the whole family or team can see and edit, with a chat-like memo thread on each event so plans and notes stay together. It also handles multiple calendars (family, work, personal) in one view. Free tier covers shared calendars fully; paid extras are mostly cosmetic themes and extra storage.

5. Business Calendar 2 — best for power users who want control

Business Calendar 2 is the pick for people who want a dense, customisable agenda: configurable views, templates for recurring events, drag-and-drop rescheduling, and local calendars that do not need a cloud account at all. The free version is usable, with a one-time pro upgrade for the full feature set — no subscription required.

Our verdict: install Google Calendar first — it is free, syncs everywhere, and does everything most people need. Pick TimeTree instead if the whole point is a shared family schedule, and check Samsung Calendar before installing anything if you own a Galaxy phone. Avoid calendar apps that demand a subscription just to show a widget or set a reminder — those basics should be free.
