5 Best Free Password Manager Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated October 30, 2026 · 7 min read

Reusing one password everywhere is the single most common way accounts get stolen — one leaked site hands attackers the key to your email, bank and social accounts. We tested the most popular free password managers on Android for one question: which one keeps your logins safe and actually fills them in when you need them, without pushing you into a subscription?

What we tested

  • Autofill that works — fills logins in apps and in Chrome without constant copy-paste
  • Free-tier honesty — what is genuinely free, and what quietly requires a subscription
  • Security basics — encryption, biometric unlock, and whether your vault is stored in the cloud or on your phone
  • Everyday use — importing existing passwords, password generator, and how easy it is to switch phones

1. Bitwarden — best free password manager, full stop

Bitwarden's free tier is the most generous we found: unlimited passwords and unlimited devices, with sync across Android, desktop and browsers, all open-source. Autofill works reliably in apps and browsers, the password generator is built in, and biometric unlock keeps daily use painless. The paid tier adds extras like file attachments and emergency access, but nothing a normal user needs on day one.

2. Proton Pass — best for privacy-first users

From the makers of Proton Mail, Proton Pass pairs a clean design with strong privacy defaults: end-to-end encrypted vaults, email-alias support that hides your real address from sign-ups, and a free tier generous enough for everyday use. If you already use Proton services, this is the natural pick.

3. KeePassDX — best fully offline option

KeePassDX is open-source and stores your vault as a single encrypted file on your own phone — no cloud account, no server, no subscription of any kind. The trade-off is real: syncing between devices is your job (via your own cloud folder or USB), and the interface is functional rather than pretty. For users who want zero cloud involvement, nothing beats it.

4. Google Password Manager — best if you want zero setup

Already built into Android and Chrome, Google Password Manager saves and autofills passwords with nothing to install, and it can check saved passwords against known data breaches. It is less flexible than a dedicated manager (no secure notes or file storage at the same level), but for someone who will never install a separate app, it is a genuine step up from reusing passwords.

5. NordPass — best design, with a free-tier catch

NordPass looks great and its autofill and password health checks are polished. The catch is the free tier: it works on only one device at a time, so it suits someone who lives on a single phone. If you switch between phone and laptop, one of the free options above will serve you better.

Our verdict: install Bitwarden first — its free tier has no device limit and no password limit, which is exactly what a password manager should offer. Only pick KeePassDX if you specifically want your vault off the cloud, and avoid any "free" manager that locks autofill or export behind a paywall — those are the two features that matter most, and locking them defeats the point.
