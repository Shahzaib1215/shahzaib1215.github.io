5 Best Free Note-Taking Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated October 25, 2026 · 7 min read

A good notes app is the one you actually open. We compared the most popular free note-taking apps on a mid-range Android phone for one question: which one captures an idea fastest — and still lets you find it a month later?

What we tested

  • Speed of capture — how fast a new note opens, including widgets and reminders
  • Organization — labels, folders, search, checklists and pins
  • Sync & offline access — notes available without internet, and across devices
  • Free-tier honesty — what is genuinely free, and what quietly needs a subscription

1. Google Keep — best for most people

Keep is the fastest note app here: open, type, done. Colour-coded notes, labels, pins, checklists, location and time reminders, and voice notes that transcribe to text. It syncs with your Google account, works offline, and has a genuinely useful home-screen widget. If you just want to remember things, start here and stop looking.

2. Microsoft OneNote — best for long, structured notes

OneNote feels like a digital binder: notebooks, sections and pages, with free-form placement of text, images and drawings. It is the strongest free pick for students and office work, especially if you already use a Microsoft account. It is heavier than Keep, so it is overkill for a two-line shopping list.

3. Notion — best for projects, not just notes

Notion turns notes into databases, task boards and wikis — great if your "notes" are actually a study plan, a content calendar or a small project. The free plan covers personal use well. The trade-off: it needs an internet connection for the smoothest experience, and it is slower than Keep for a one-second capture.

4. Obsidian — best for private, offline notes

Obsidian stores your notes as plain Markdown files on your own device — no account required, no cloud lock-in, and your notes stay readable in any text editor forever. Its graph view and community plugins suit people building a personal knowledge base. Syncing across devices is the catch: the official sync is paid, so free users sync via their own cloud folder instead.

5. Samsung Notes — best if you own a Samsung phone

If your phone shipped with Samsung Notes, check it before installing anything: handwriting with an S Pen, PDF annotation, audio-synced notes and Samsung Cloud sync are already there, free and ad-free. The catch is obvious — it is only a real option on Samsung devices.

Our verdict: install Google Keep first — it is free, fast and does what most people need from a notes app. Move to OneNote only if your notes are long documents, to Notion if they are projects, and to Obsidian if keeping your notes as your own offline files matters most to you. Avoid "free" notes apps that lock basic features like search or export behind a subscription.
