5 Best Free VPN Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated October 28, 2026 · 7 min read

Free VPNs are a minefield: some are genuinely useful, some throttle you into uselessness, and some quietly pay for their "free" tier with your data. We looked at the most popular free VPN apps on Android and asked one question: which free VPN can you actually use every day — on public Wi-Fi, without selling your privacy to pay for it?

What we checked

  • Free-tier limits — data caps, speed limits, and how many server locations you get
  • Privacy policy — no-logs claims, and whether the company behind it is trustworthy
  • Ads & tracking — a VPN that shows ads or tracks you defeats its own purpose
  • Everyday use — one-tap connect, kill switch, and battery drain on a mid-range phone

1. Proton VPN — best free VPN, full stop

Proton VPN's free tier is the rare one with no data cap and no ads — the same company that makes Proton Mail, with open-source apps and a published no-logs policy. The trade-off is honest: fewer server locations than the paid plan, and free servers can get crowded at peak hours. For daily protection on public Wi-Fi, this is the one to install first.

2. Windscribe — best free VPN for occasional use

Windscribe's free tier gives you a monthly data allowance and a decent choice of server locations, plus a built-in ad blocker. It is a solid pick if you only need a VPN a few times a week — but heavy daily users will hit the data cap fast.

3. TunnelBear — best free VPN for beginners

TunnelBear is the friendliest VPN app on Android: big friendly bear animations, one-tap connect, and a clear privacy policy from an independently audited company. Its free tier comes with a small monthly data allowance, so treat it as a trial or a travel backup, not a daily driver.

4. Psiphon — best free VPN for blocked networks

Psiphon is built for a different job: getting past network blocks and censorship rather than everyday privacy. It is free with ads, and it is the tool many people reach for when nothing else connects. Just know what it is — a circumvention tool first, a privacy VPN second.

5. Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 (WARP) — best for speed, with a catch

WARP is free, unlimited, and very fast — but it is not a traditional VPN: it encrypts your traffic and routes it through Cloudflare, yet it does not let you pick a server country to unblock region-locked content. Great for securing café Wi-Fi at full speed; wrong tool if you need a different location.

Free VPNs to avoid

Skip any free VPN that has no clear company behind it, no published privacy policy, or a business model it cannot explain. If a VPN is free, unlimited, and ad-free from an unknown developer, your data is almost certainly the product. Stick to the names above — or pay for a reputable one if you need more servers and speed.

Our verdict: install Proton VPN first — it is the only free tier here with no data cap, no ads, and a trustworthy company behind it. Use Windscribe or TunnelBear as backups, and keep Psiphon in your pocket for networks that block everything else. And remember: a VPN protects your connection, not your identity — it does not make you anonymous online.
